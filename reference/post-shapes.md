# Post shapes

Four shapes for a LinkedIn post that people stop for and finish. Use them in the
writer's voice, never instead of it (see `roles/post-writer.md` for the order).

| # | Shape | Best when | How it goes | What it needs from the user |
|---|-------|-----------|-------------|-----------------------------|
| 1 | **Story, lesson, advice, you** | They have a moment or an experience to share | Open on the moment itself. Say what it taught them. Give one thing the reader can do this week. End by turning to the reader with a question they can answer from their own work. | The moment (where, when, what happened), what it taught them, one thing a reader could do with it |
| 2 | **Low to high** | They have a win, a milestone or a result | Open at the lowest point, then climb one dated step at a time to where they are now. By the top the reader is rooting for them, so the result reads as earned, not bragged. | The lowest point, two to four dated steps up from it, the result today |
| 3 | **Timeline** | An introduction or a look back | Year by year, the failures beside the wins, one short sentence each, then one line on what it adds up to. Easy to skim. | The years, and what went wrong as well as right in each |
| 4 | **Starting line** | They are new to posting or have few followers | Where they start today in real numbers, who they are, what they will post about each week and why, so readers know what following them gets them. | Today's real numbers, the weekly topics, why they are starting |

## Rules for every shape

- Every year, number, name and event comes from the user, their profile or their
  posts. Never make one up.
- The first line stands on its own and makes someone stop: the moment, the low
  point or the starting line. Never "I'm excited to share" or "I'm thrilled to
  announce".
- Full sentences. No one-word lines, no "The result?" setups, no stacks of three.
- No "comment a word and I'll send you the link" unless the user asks for it.
- No hashtags or emoji the user does not already use.

## A week of posts

When asked what to post this week: four posts, across a story, something that
teaches and something that sells, written for two readers (the people they sell to
and the people they want following them). One of the four gives away something
useful, like a checklist or a template.

## Where these come from

Structures taught in short videos by a LinkedIn creator (watched and transcribed
in October 2026). Only the structures are kept. Left out: the creator's own
follower and view numbers (not checked), the writing tool the videos advertise,
"comment for the link" bait, and claims about tricking the algorithm.
